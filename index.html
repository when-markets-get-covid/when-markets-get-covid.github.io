Abstract

Pricing the risk of a spreading virus

Journal Journal of Financial Economics, 157, 103850 (July 2024)
DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2024.103850
Keywords Asset prices, pandemic risk, medical announcements, text analysis
Editor Nikolai Roussanov

We quantify the exposure of major financial markets to news shocks about global contagion risk while accounting for local epidemic conditions. For a wide cross section of countries, we construct a novel dataset comprising (i) announcements related to COVID-19 and (ii) high-frequency data on epidemic news diffused through Twitter. We provide novel empirical evidence about financial dynamics both around epidemic announcements and at daily/intra-daily frequencies. Analysis of contagion data and social media activity about COVID-19 suggest that the market price of contagion risk is significant.

Main findings

Three things the data say

Around official announcements, at a daily frequency, and hour by hour, contagion news moves equity and bond markets in consistent ways.

01 · Announcements
Equities jump

Medical news helps equities

In a ±60-minute window, equities appreciate right after COVID-19 announcements in both advanced and emerging economies. Bonds dip slightly in advanced economies and don't move in emerging ones. That's consistent with investors who value the early resolution of uncertainty.

02 · Market price of risk
≥ 0.3%

Contagion risk is priced

A conditional model with contagion-driven betas gives a large, significant market price of risk for global contagion news. 0.3% per day is a reasonable lower bound. Daily and hourly Twitter-based news give the same answer.

03 · Risk premia
+28% / +13%

A crisis-sized shock to premia

At the April 2020 peak, daily equity risk premia may have risen 28% in advanced and 13% in emerging economies above their sample medians. That's comparable to the global financial crisis. In emerging markets, high-COVID bonds work as insurance.
