Study 2

Announcements study

We look at bond and equity returns around COVID-19 medical announcements, from 60 minutes before to 60 minutes after. The data cover 15,839 hand-collected announcements in 20 countries: case reports, live-streamed press conferences, and speeches by presidents and prime ministers.

Why announcements should matter

A simple model of asset demand

Take an investor with Epstein–Zin preferences who chooses bonds \(B\) and stocks \(S\), where equity payouts carry risk \(\theta\) with variance \(\sigma^2\). With unit elasticity of intertemporal substitution, the demand for equities is

$$\frac{p(S)}{p(B)} \;=\; E_0[\theta] \;-\; (\gamma-1)\,V_0[\theta]\cdot S, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \frac{\partial p(S)}{\partial \sigma^2} < 0 \;\;\text{if}\;\; \gamma > 1.$$

An announcement is an unbiased signal about \(\theta\) that lowers the posterior variance \(\sigma^2\). If investors care about when uncertainty is resolved (\(\gamma>1\)), announcements should on average move demand from bonds to equities. Equity prices should rise. Bond prices should stay flat if bond supply is flat, and dip if it slopes upward.

To test this, we compare the minute-by-minute behavior of each asset around announcements with its behavior at the same clock time before the epidemic (\(Z_t\)). We fit a quadratic in time that is allowed to break at the announcement time \(t^*\):

$$Z_t = \big(c_{pre} + c_{t>t^*}\big) + \big(\alpha_{pre} + \alpha_{t>t^*}\big)\,t + \big(\beta_{pre} + \beta_{t>t^*}\big)\,t^2, \qquad t\in[\,t^*\pm 60\,]$$

Standard errors are HAC-adjusted. A country's epidemic period starts on the day it passes 100 confirmed cases.

Result 1

In both advanced (AE) and emerging (EE) economies, announcements help equities during the epidemic.

These results are not a peso problem. They hold when we use only announcements that carry bad news, when we drop days with news in the top or bottom 1%, and in a sample that ends in December 2020.

The dataset

15,839 announcements, time-stamped to the minute

For each country we took the official press statements from the Ministry of Health website. When a statement had no official timestamp, we used the Twitter account of the Ministry or another government body, and if that failed, major local newspapers. Monetary and fiscal policy announcements are excluded. The dataset is public.
